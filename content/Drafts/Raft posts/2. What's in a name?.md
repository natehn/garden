notes on the word raft from *An Exaltation of Larks*: 
- "A rafter of turkeys": Probably not what you think, if you see birds sitting on a beam. The term is related to *raft* in the sense of "a large and often motley collection of people and things, as a *raft* of books," according to Webster. It is also related to *raff*, which means a collection of things, and appears in some interesting variations in *riffraff* and *raffish*. 
- "A riffraff of knaves": This term appears in the *St. Albans* list as *a Rafull of knauys*. The OED (Oxford English Dictionary) refers you from "rafull" or "rayfull" to "raffle," and we are back to our root *raff*, obviously a very popular word in the fifteenth century. This time we are told that "raffle" has as one of its meanings *riffraff*, and we have our translation.

## Acronym or *back*ronym?

Despite the language on the website about "resilience, autonomy, fullness, and transparency," Raft is not actually an acronym - those were added later. 

Accordingly, Raft should not be all-caps, and should be referred to as either Raft Foundation Inc., Raft Foundation, or just Raft.
