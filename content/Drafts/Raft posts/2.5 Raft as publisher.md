Fiscal sponsors are more similar to publishers than one might think. Like publishers, we receive applications *constantly* from “creators” - if the reader will allow me to stretch that term somewhat - asking us to house and support their work. While we do not “sell” or promote their creations, we do, by hosting them, in a sense endorse them, and commit to providing significant support over a potentially-indefinite period of time. 

And so when I started digging into Robert Baxter’s commonplace meta-publication, *Some Notes on Publishing and Education*, I found it quite easy to port his bibliographic dialogue over to the questions I have been asking at Raft. Just as he has been slowly developing a press, so too am I developing a platform of sorts.

Below, find a series of excerpts, narrowed down from Baxter’s selection,[^origin] that I felt applied to Raft’s situation, followed by additional commentary from yours truly.

[^origin]: Some of these excerpts are actually third-hand, pulled by Baxter from yet another compendium. I’ll let the interested reader buy Baxter’s zine - which I highly recommend - to obtain the full provenance, including page numbers from the original source.

There were stories, mythological and historical; there were songs and proverbs and parables. There were also big cycles of stories, chains of stories. You could call them *books*, if you can get past the idea that a book has to be a material object.[^64]

[^64]: Robert Bringhurst, *What Is Reading For?*
