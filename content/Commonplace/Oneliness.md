Remove the “l” from loneliness and you are left with “oneliness,” an older precursor meaning simply *the condition of being alone*.

Loneliness carries so much baggage. We often treat it as something to be avoided. We act as if being alone can only be the result of a lack of social cohesion. (Introverts, I’m told, know better.) We forget that [*everyone* is lonely](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XRT5jYX5jFc).

This note is named “oneliness” aspirationally. I aspire to a calm, centered, sensitive, confident presence of self that pervades no matter whether I am alone or with others. That begins from a place of being alone and then invites, embraces, inspires, transforms. 

Not just for me, but for all of us.

A few years ago, I took a solo trip to [[Travel log#Iceland|Iceland]]. It was my first major trip since splitting up with a long-term partner and I was excited to dip back into the experience of connecting with other travelers, leaning into spontaneity. 

The experience in Reykjavik was of this ilk - I met folks at the hostel, went on a day trip with them. But as I began my 7-day loop around the country’s ring road, I found myself deeply, challengingly alone. Everyone I met along the way was on another schedule, or going the opposite direction (even hitchhikers I tried to pick up were headed the other way). And amidst this lack of connection, I clung to my own schedule like it was all I had. 

I wrote in a journal:

> A terrible feeling: lonely but not alone.

There were many fun times, and I did form a few relationships - I even met up with one couple back in New York to watch a soccer game. But the experience came nowhere close to my expectations, and shifted the way I have approached travel since. 

Rather than a schedule, rather than ticking every box, I ask, how will I best connect with other people? How can I make space for the unknown, for new experiences - for the unexpected?

When I look back on the trip now, the first thing I think of is the loneliness. But the *second* thing I think of is my one true spontaneous moment. On the very first morning, driving the opposite direction from town, seeking adventure, and finding it: a long walk to the glowing lava of an active volcano, it sulferous fumes engulfing me, its heat on my skin.
