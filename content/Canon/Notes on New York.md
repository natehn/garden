## For visitors

Here are some options to consider for your visit in Manhattan, beyond the classic skyscrapers, Broadway shows, and museums:

- Any of the parks will do, but of course Central Park. I like the rambler and the conservatory garden, but just exploring is worth it. Washington Square Park is a little much but is a great place to pick up on the NYC energy, for better or worse. If you go there, make sure to also drop by the Music Inn
- I love to get cronuts at the original Donut Pub at 7th and 14th
- The High Line is both oft-recommended and a must-do if you are in Manhattan on a nice day
- Earth Room, Broken Kilometer, and Dream House are all great long-term art installations (before you start, check out Mercer Street Books as well)
- Explore community gardens in the Lower East Side, then check out the Museum of Reclaimed Urban Space. Alphabet City has some good stuff - Bullet Space, The Library, Mast Books, and more, and a good spot for a notably-creative and reasonably-priced sandwich in the area is Sunny & Annie’s deli
- Also nearby, on a cold day, go to the Russian & Turkish Baths, then check out Lady Wong and Eat Village Books nearby, and close things out with some sake at Accidental Bar
- Get some hand-pulled noodles on Doyers Street in Chinatown, then get dessert in Little Italy, or on a hot day, ice cream at the Original Chinatown Ice Cream Factory
- Or stay in Chinatown and get some cheap skewers at the BBQ cart at Grand and Crystal (unless it rains - then go to Vanessa’s Dumpling House and pray for a table)
- See some weird music at Downtown Music Gallery
- If you go to Times Square, check out Max Neuhaus’s drone art installation underneath one of the sewer grates, you might need to bend down to hear it
- If you do go to a Broadway show, my favorite nearby food is Cha Pa’s for some good pho
- If you are in midtown, my favorite area is K-town, and Food Gallery 32 is my go-to there. Kentuckians will also want to check out Jack Demsey’s, the UK bar
- Going uptown to check out the Met Cloisters is highly recommended (and be prepared to walk up the hill)…and it could be worth it to get some Lighthouse seafood on the way up or back

There’s honestly *so much* but hopefully this is a nice start. Don’t even get me started on restaurants.

Outside of Manhattan, some highlights:

- Getting food in in the Flushing and Jackson Heights neighborhoods of Queens
- The best cheap date I ever organized was in Williamsburg: Oasis mediterranean, Spectacle theater movie, then a drink at Duff’s
- John Cushing big band at Fiction Bar/Cafe, if you’re there on the right night
- The Rockaway beaches, the Coney Island Boardwalk, Brooklyn Botanical Garden + Prospect PArk
- Walking around Greenwood Cemetery (parrots! look it up) then watching the sunset at Sunset Park
- Trinidad Golden Place
- Bed Stuy Aquarium (not a real acquarium)
- Issue Project Room
- McCarren Park is a favorite, and Diamond Lil is a good spot near-ish by - Paulie Gee’s, too
- Beacon (DIA!) and Cold Spring, if you want to get out of the city (Bull Hill, Bear Mountain, and Breakneck Ridge hikes)
- Governor’s Island is a day trip but is fun when they have stuff going on in the summer

Some other random tips:

- Get pizza at pretty much any slice shop, just look at the Google rating and avoid ones that focus on the price
- For late night food that can accommodate a larger group without a long wait, try Dim Sum Palace
- For when you need to use the bathroom, after 5pm: walk into a bar, go straight to the back, use the bathroom
- For when you are looking for someplace to hang after dark that *isn’t* a bar: Qahwah House (or the Chai Spot if you are in Lower Manhattan)
- If you like mystery novels, go to the Mysterious Bookshop, if you like romances to to the Ripped Bodice, if you like zines go to Quimby’s, if you like cookbooks go to Bonne Slotnick

## NYC housing

Housing in the city is [notoriously difficult](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/01/22/nyregion/housing-costs-nyc.html). The best way to find a good spot, especially on the cheaper end of things, is to talk to people. The next-best place is probably still Craigslist, the Listings Project, then Facebook groups and Streeteasy. Or roaming around the neighborhood you want to rent in, looking at signs - though that seems to happen less and less.

![[nyc-flowershop.jpeg|A flatiron-shaped building with a flowershop's name written down its face.]]

When I was first was looking for a place alone in NYC, I looked into co-housing options. Joining an intentional community Facebook group is probably the most effective option. But some others I looked at, which were generally more expensive, included SharedEasy and Outsite.

![[nyc-bar.jpeg|A handwritten notice on the wall of a bar asking patrons not to leave trash on the street.]]
