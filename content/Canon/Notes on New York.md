## For visitors

Here are some options to consider for your visit in Manhattan, beyond the classic skyscrapers, Broadway shows, and museums:

- If you just walk along Broadway between Columbus Circle and Chinatown, or even just between Herald Square and Chinatown, then swing down into the financial district, you’ll get a good sampling (Herald Square, Flatiron, Times Square, etc. - and this intersects with many of the things elsewhere on this list as well)
- Explore community gardens in the Lower East Side, then check out the Museum of Reclaimed Urban Space. Alphabet City has some good stuff - Bullet Space, The Library, Mast Books, and more, and a good spot for a notably-creative and reasonably-priced sandwich in the area is Sunny & Annie’s deli
- Also nearby, on a cold day, go to the Russian & Turkish Baths, then check out Lady Wong and East Village Books nearby, and close things out with some sake at Accidental Bar
- Get some hand-pulled noodles on Doyers Street in Chinatown, then get dessert in Little Italy, or on a hot day, ice cream at the Original Chinatown Ice Cream Factory
- Chinatown is really the best. For example, in the evening or even after a show, get some cheap skewers at the BBQ cart at Grand and Crystal (unless it rains) Or for dinner, go to Vanessa’s Dumpling House and pray for a table
- See some weird music at Downtown Music Gallery
- Any of the parks will do, and Central Park is great but can really eat up your day. If you do, like the rambler and the conservatory garden, but just exploring is worth it. Smaller parks can definitely serve through out the day, though, like Tompkins Square, which is near some of the other things listed here. Washington Square Park is a little much but is a great place to pick up on the NYC energy, for better or worse (if you go there, make sure to also drop by the Music Inn)
- I love to get cronuts at the original Donut Pub at 7th and 14th
- The High Line is both oft-recommended and a must-do if you are in Manhattan on a nice day
- Another fun walk is along Greenwich Ave between the Whitney and Washington Square Park. This can be tacked on to the end of a southbound walk on the High Line
- Earth Room, Broken Kilometer, and Dream House are all great long-term art installations (before you start, check out Mercer Street Books as well)
- If you are in midtown (and I have to say, it is largely missable), my favorite area is K-town, and Food Gallery 32 is my go-to there. Kentuckians will also want to check out Jack Demsey’s, the UK bar
- If you go to Times Square, check out Max Neuhaus’s drone art installation underneath one of the sewer grates, you might need to bend down to hear it
- If you do go to a Broadway show, my favorite nearby food is Cha Pa’s for some good pho
- Staten Island Ferry at sunset is also a classic, 40 minutes round trip, pretty good view of the Statue of Liberty and Lower Manhattan
- Going uptown to check out the Met Cloisters is highly recommended (and be prepared to walk up the hill)…and it could be worth it to get some Lighthouse seafood on the way up or back

> *From a friend of a friend:* Chinatown is not to be missed. Specifically a Veg Curry Mee Soup ay New West Malay. [Ed: this is near the Chinatown ice cream place I mention above.] Then go to nearby Columbus Park and sit watching elderly musicians perform old Mao hymns. 

There’s honestly *so much* but hopefully this is a nice start for Manhattan. Don’t even get me started on restaurants.

Outside of Manhattan, some highlights:

- Getting food in in the Flushing and Jackson Heights neighborhoods of Queens
- The best cheap date I ever organized was in Williamsburg: Oasis mediterranean, Spectacle theater movie, then a drink at Duff’s
- John Cushing big band at Fiction Bar/Cafe, if you’re there on the right night
- The Rockaway beaches, the Coney Island Boardwalk, Brooklyn Botanical Garden + walking in Prospect Park
- Walking around Greenwood Cemetery (parrots! look it up) then watching the sunset at Sunset Park
- Trinidad Golden Place
- Bed Stuy Aquarium (not a real acquarium)
- Issue Project Room
- McCarren Park is a favorite, and Diamond Lil is a good spot near-ish by - Paulie Gee’s, too
- Beacon (DIA!) and Cold Spring, if you want to get out of the city (Bull Hill, Bear Mountain, and Breakneck Ridge hikes)
- Governor’s Island is a day trip but is fun when they have stuff going on in the summer, especially if you rent a bike or bring your own

## NYC housing

Finding quality, [affordable](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/01/22/nyregion/housing-costs-nyc.html) housing in the city is [notoriously difficult](https://hellgatenyc.com/my-months-long-hunt-for-a-rent-stabilized-apartment-nyc/). The best way to find a good spot, especially on the cheaper end of things, is to talk to people. The next-best place is probably still Craigslist, the [Listings Project](https://www.listingsproject.com/), [Gertie’s](https://gertieslivework.com/), [Polish craigslist](https://bazarynka.com), then Facebook groups and Streeteasy. Or roaming around the neighborhood you want to rent in, looking at signs - though that seems to happen less and less.

When checking out an apartment, check the water press, that there is hot water, ask about bed bugs, the windows and heating, if heat comes up from lower apartments, etc., and if there have been any repairs recently. Use the resources below (for rent increases) to see also if the landlord is a good one, and how many other buildings they own.

If you plan to stay long-term, [affordable housing lotteries](https://hcr.ny.gov/lotteries) are also worth considering.

![[nyc-flowershop.jpeg|A flatiron-shaped building with a flowershop's name written down its face.]]

When I was first was looking for a place alone in NYC, I looked into co-housing options. Joining an intentional community Facebook group is probably the most effective option. But some others I looked at, which were generally more expensive, included SharedEasy and Outsite.

### Housing issues

You’ve found a place, it’s been a year, and your rent is going up. How do you know the increase is legal? Or how do you know whether the way improvements/repairs are being handled is legal? Here are some resources:

- [Am I Rent Stabilized?](https://amirentstabilized.com/) ([unfortunately](https://hellgatenyc.com/landlords-went-wild-history-of-vacancy-deregulation-in-new-york/) these [are decreasing](https://www.thecityreporter.nyc/2023/03/01/ridgewood-rent-stabilization-substantial-rehabilitation/), but still the majority)
- [Who Owns What](https://whoownswhat.justfix.org/en/)
- [Rent Guidelines Board](https://rentguidelinesboard.cityofnewyork.us/)
- [Rent Regulation Records Access](https://hcr.ny.gov/records-access) ([stabilized building lists](https://rentguidelinesboard.cityofnewyork.us/resources/rent-stabilized-building-lists/))
- [Displacement Alert Portal](https://portal.displacementalert.org/)
- [Good Cause Calculator](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/18ORHC-ZSwTKpF9YZswtIlmzB6kvD0Tc2SbTmEXPT-SI/edit?usp=sharing)

And if you run into trouble: 

- [Call 311](https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-01074) and complain about every apartment issue, daily
- [Brooklyn Eviction Defense](https://www.brooklynevictiondefense.com/)
- [More resources](https://docs.google.com/document/d/113IGSbTD2UKfJWx7z1g4lG05gkxS3IsrYJ2lCGUAkFQ/edit?tab=t.0)
- [HG&J](https://hmgdjlaw.com/)
